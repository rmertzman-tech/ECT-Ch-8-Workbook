1. Arrive

Tools can support agency, but they can also capture it.

In Chapter 8, you learned that technology is not just a thing we use. A tool can route attention, shape choices, invite shortcuts, hide context, or open repair. This workbook helps you audit that route before deciding whether the tool is supporting or capturing agency.

Privacy reminder: You may use the provided case, a fictional example, a public example, a historical example, a composite example, or a low-stakes personal example. Do not disclose private, identifying, or sensitive information.
2. Read the Case

The AI-Written Email

A student needs to send a difficult email to a professor about a missed deadline. The student uses an AI tool to draft the message. The draft sounds polished and confident, but it includes details that are exaggerated, skips responsibility for the missed work, and uses a tone the student would not normally choose. The student can send it immediately, revise it, or start over.

5. Support vs Capture

Decide whether agency is being supported or captured.

Tool support: the tool helps the person act more responsibly.
Agency capture: the tool redirects or replaces the person’s attention, judgment, or authorship.
6. Data and Model Limits

Treat the model as an instrument, not an authority.

Data reduction: a system may simplify people, contexts, and values into what can be measured or predicted.
